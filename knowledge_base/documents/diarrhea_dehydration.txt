Diarrhea and Dehydration

Diarrhea means passing loose or watery stools more often than usual. It's commonly caused by viral or bacterial infections, often from contaminated food or water, and usually resolves on its own within a few days. The main danger from diarrhea isn't the diarrhea itself but the dehydration (fluid loss) it can cause, especially in young children and the elderly.

Home care focuses on replacing lost fluids and salts. Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) is the most effective home treatment — it's a simple mix of clean water, salt, and sugar in the correct ratio (pre-made ORS sachets from a pharmacy are ideal and inexpensive; if unavailable, a home mix of 6 level teaspoons of sugar and half a teaspoon of salt in 1 liter of clean, boiled-and-cooled water can help, though a pre-made sachet is safer and more accurate). Sips of ORS should be given frequently, even if vomiting occurs — small frequent sips are better absorbed than large amounts at once. Continue normal breastfeeding for infants. Keep eating light, easily digestible food as tolerated (bananas, rice, plain khichri) — there's no need to fast completely. Avoid sugary sodas or juices as a rehydration substitute, since too much sugar can worsen diarrhea.

Good hygiene helps prevent spread and further episodes: wash hands with soap after using the toilet and before eating or preparing food, and drink only clean or boiled water.

Warning signs that need urgent medical care: signs of dehydration such as very little or no urination, dark urine, dry mouth, sunken eyes, extreme thirst, or in babies, no wet diaper for 6+ hours or a sunken soft spot on the head; blood in the stool; diarrhea lasting more than 2-3 days without improvement; high fever alongside diarrhea; repeated vomiting that prevents keeping fluids down; severe abdominal pain; or confusion and extreme weakness. Diarrhea in a very young infant, an elderly person, or someone with an existing serious illness should be treated more urgently even before these signs appear, since dehydration progresses faster in these groups. This information is educational only and does not replace evaluation by a health worker, especially if warning signs are present.
