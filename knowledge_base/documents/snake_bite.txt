Snake Bite

A snake bite is always a medical emergency until proven otherwise, even if the person feels okay at first — venom effects can take time to appear, and it's not possible to reliably tell if a bite was venomous just by how it looks or feels in the first few minutes.

What to do immediately: move away from the snake so it or another snake cannot bite again — do not try to kill, catch, or handle it. Keep the bitten person as calm and still as possible; movement can help spread venom faster through the body. Remove rings, bangles, watches, or tight clothing near the bite before swelling starts. Gently wash the bite with soap and water if available. Loosely wrap the bitten limb with a clean bandage or cloth without cutting off blood flow, and keep the limb immobilized and roughly level with the heart. Arrange the fastest possible transport to the nearest hospital or health facility that can provide treatment — this is the single most important step.

If a photo of the snake can be taken safely from a distance without any delay or risk, it may help health workers identify the right treatment, but this should never delay getting to a hospital.

What NOT to do, because these traditional methods have been shown to cause more harm than benefit: do not apply a tight tourniquet, do not cut the wound, do not try to suck out the venom by mouth, do not apply ice, and do not use traditional herbal remedies in place of medical care. Do not give the person alcohol or caffeine. For pain, paracetamol is generally acceptable, but avoid aspirin or ibuprofen-type medicines, since these can increase bleeding risk.

Watch for and be ready to respond to: vomiting (turn the person onto their side to prevent choking), difficulty breathing, or loss of consciousness — these require close monitoring on the way to care.

This is an emergency situation: the guidance above is first aid only, meant to keep the person as safe as possible while getting them to a hospital as fast as possible, not a treatment in itself. Antivenom and proper medical care can only be given at a health facility.
