High Blood Pressure (Hypertension)

Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing against the walls of the arteries as the heart pumps. High blood pressure, or hypertension, means this force stays higher than normal over time. It is very common and, importantly, usually causes no symptoms at all — which is why it's sometimes called a "silent" condition, and why regular checks matter even when someone feels fine.

Over years, uncontrolled high blood pressure can quietly damage the heart, blood vessels, kidneys, and eyes, and significantly raises the risk of heart attack and stroke. This is why managing it, even without symptoms, is worthwhile.

Lifestyle changes that genuinely help lower blood pressure: reducing salt in cooking and avoiding very salty snacks or pickles; eating more vegetables, fruits, and whole grains; maintaining a healthy body weight; staying physically active with regular activity like brisk walking; limiting or avoiding tobacco, paan, gutka, and alcohol; and managing stress where possible. These changes help even for people already taking blood pressure medicine — medicine and lifestyle work together, not one instead of the other.

If a doctor has prescribed blood pressure medicine, it's important to take it consistently, even on days the person feels fine, and not stop it without medical advice — stopping suddenly can be dangerous.

Some symptoms, though not always present, can occur with very high blood pressure or its complications: severe headache, chest pain, shortness of breath, vision problems, or nosebleeds.

When to seek medical care urgently: a severe headache with blurred vision, chest pain, difficulty breathing, confusion, or numbness/weakness on one side of the body (which can be signs of a hypertensive emergency or stroke) — these need immediate emergency care. Otherwise, anyone with risk factors (overweight, family history, high salt diet, older age) should get their blood pressure checked periodically even without symptoms. This information is educational only and does not replace regular blood pressure monitoring and medical advice.
