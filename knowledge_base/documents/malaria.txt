Malaria

Malaria is a disease caused by a parasite spread through the bite of infected mosquitoes, mostly active from dusk to dawn. It's common in many parts of Pakistan, especially in warmer, humid areas and after monsoon rains when mosquito breeding increases.

Common symptoms include recurring fever (which may come in cycles — high fever followed by sweating and a return to normal, then repeating), chills and shivering, headache, muscle and joint pain, fatigue, nausea, and sometimes vomiting. Because these symptoms overlap heavily with many other common illnesses like flu, typhoid, or dengue, malaria cannot be reliably identified by symptoms alone — a blood test at a clinic or lab is needed to confirm it and rule out other causes, which matters because treatment differs depending on the actual illness.

While waiting to reach a clinic, general supportive care is reasonable: rest, drink plenty of fluids, and use paracetamol for fever and discomfort. However, malaria requires specific antimalarial medicine prescribed after diagnosis — home remedies or general fever medicine alone will not cure malaria if that is the actual cause, and delaying proper diagnosis and treatment can allow the illness to become more severe.

Prevention is a meaningful part of protecting against malaria: sleeping under insecticide-treated mosquito nets, using mosquito repellent, wearing long sleeves in the evening in high-risk areas, and removing standing water around the home (which is where mosquitoes breed) all reduce risk.

When to seek medical care: any recurring or unexplained fever, especially with chills and sweating, should be tested rather than assumed to be a common cold — this is especially important in areas where malaria is common. Seek urgent care immediately if fever is accompanied by confusion, difficulty breathing, repeated vomiting, seizures, severe weakness, unconsciousness, or yellowing of the eyes/skin (jaundice), as these can indicate a severe, life-threatening form of malaria requiring emergency treatment. This information is educational only; malaria always needs laboratory confirmation and treatment prescribed by a qualified health worker.
