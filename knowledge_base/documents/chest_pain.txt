Chest Pain

Chest pain can have many causes, ranging from minor and harmless (like muscle strain, indigestion, or acid reflux) to very serious and life-threatening (like a heart attack). Because it is impossible to reliably tell the difference based on how the pain feels alone — and because some serious causes can worsen quickly — sudden or severe chest pain should always be treated as a possible emergency until a medical professional rules out serious causes.

Certain features make a heart-related cause more likely and should never be ignored: pain or pressure/tightness in the center or left side of the chest, pain spreading to the arm (especially the left), jaw, neck, or back, chest pain along with shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, dizziness, or a feeling of impending doom, and chest pain brought on by exertion that eases with rest. In someone with risk factors like older age, diabetes, high blood pressure, smoking, or a family history of heart disease, chest pain deserves extra caution.

Less dangerous causes are more likely if the pain is sharp and worsens with a specific movement or when pressing on the chest, is clearly linked to recent heavy meals (suggesting indigestion or reflux), or is reproducible by pressing on a specific spot (suggesting a muscle strain). However, this cannot be used to confidently rule out a serious cause without medical evaluation, especially the first time someone experiences chest pain like this.

What to do: for sudden, severe, or new chest pain — especially with any of the warning features above — treat it as a medical emergency. Have the person sit or rest in a comfortable position, stay calm, and get to the nearest hospital or emergency service as fast as possible; do not wait to see if it passes on its own, and do not attempt to drive yourself if symptoms are severe. Do not try to self-diagnose or delay care to "wait and see," since time matters greatly in heart-related emergencies.

This information is educational only and is deliberately not a diagnostic tool — its only purpose is to help someone recognize when chest pain needs immediate emergency medical attention rather than home management.
