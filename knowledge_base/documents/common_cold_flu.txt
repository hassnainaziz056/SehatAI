Common Cold and Flu

Both the common cold and the flu (influenza) are illnesses caused by viruses that affect the nose, throat, and airways. They spread easily from person to person through coughing, sneezing, and touching contaminated surfaces.

A cold usually comes on gradually and tends to be milder: a runny or blocked nose, sneezing, mild sore throat, and mild cough. People with a cold can often still go about daily activities, though they feel unwell.

The flu usually comes on more suddenly and hits harder: high fever, chills, body aches, strong fatigue, headache, and a dry cough, sometimes alongside the nose and throat symptoms of a cold. Flu often makes a person feel too weak or tired to continue normal activities.

Home care for both is similar: rest as much as possible, drink plenty of water and fluids to stay hydrated, eat light nutritious food even if appetite is low, and use warm salt-water gargles or steam inhalation for a sore throat and congestion. A paracetamol-based medicine can help with fever and body aches when taken at the correct dose. Antibiotics do not work on colds or flu, since both are caused by viruses, not bacteria.

Most colds and flu get better on their own within one to two weeks. Cough can sometimes linger a bit longer.

When to see a doctor: fever that is very high or lasts more than 3-4 days; difficulty breathing or shortness of breath; chest pain or pressure; symptoms that improve and then suddenly get worse; a young child, elderly person, pregnant woman, or someone with an existing illness (like heart or lung disease) showing flu symptoms, since they are at higher risk of complications; or severe weakness, confusion, or inability to keep fluids down. This information is educational only and does not replace an in-person medical evaluation, especially for high-risk individuals.
