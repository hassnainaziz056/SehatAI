Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)

Paracetamol, also known as acetaminophen, is one of the most commonly used medicines for reducing fever and relieving mild to moderate pain, such as headache, body aches, toothache, and pain from colds or flu. It's widely available without a prescription and is generally considered safe when used correctly, at the right dose, for both adults and children (children need a different, weight-based dose, usually in liquid form).

Correct use matters a lot with paracetamol because the safe dose has a ceiling — taking more than the recommended amount, or combining multiple medicines that all contain paracetamol (common in cold and flu combination products) without realizing it, can seriously harm the liver. Always check the packaging or ask a pharmacist for the correct dose based on age and weight, follow the recommended spacing between doses, and never exceed the maximum daily amount stated on the package.

For most healthy adults, paracetamol taken as directed causes few side effects. Rare side effects can include allergic reactions (rash, swelling, difficulty breathing) or, with overdose, serious liver damage. People with existing liver disease, or who drink alcohol heavily, should talk to a health worker before regular use, since paracetamol is processed by the liver.

Paracetamol treats the symptom (fever or pain), not the underlying cause — if fever or pain persists or keeps returning after the medicine wears off, the underlying illness still needs proper evaluation.

When to seek medical care: if a fever doesn't improve with paracetamol, or keeps coming back over several days; if someone (especially a child) may have taken more than the recommended dose or an overdose — this is a medical emergency even if the person seems fine at first, since liver damage from overdose can take a day or more to show symptoms; or if there are signs of an allergic reaction. This information is educational only; always follow packaging instructions or a pharmacist's/doctor's guidance for the correct dose.
